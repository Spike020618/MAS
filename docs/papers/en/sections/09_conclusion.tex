\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%======================================================================

This work uses AEIC structured reports as an observable interface for leader--follower experimentation and binds ex-ante public contracts to real execution paths. A public Static-AEIC rule substantially changes the primary backend's resource actions, while collective accuracy improves under the joint contract-and-execution configuration; an outcome-only reward does not yield the same pattern. Controlled information experiments support the behavioral measurement value of AEIC-derived signals, but field ablations establish neither the independent necessity of a field nor an accuracy effect of AEIC formatting itself. Dose response, robustness suites, and frozen provenance support this bounded interpretation, and the held-out-backend failure shows that effective contracts require model-specific calibration. The next identification sequence is report-interface validation, contract/resource decomposition, and only then finite contract selection.

Accordingly, the paper identifies empirical follower response under fixed public contracts rather than proving a general Stackelberg equilibrium. Its reusable artifacts are the report schema, choice-before-execution resource enforcement, process-signal settlement, longitudinal reputation state, and recomputable traces. Future work will estimate reward--cost response surfaces on frozen tasks, define a leader objective over quality, resources, and failure risk, and select only within a finite contract space; every selected contract must be recalibrated on new model backends.
