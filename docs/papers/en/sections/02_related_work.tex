\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Sampling, Voting, and Multi-Agent Deliberation}
Early test-time scaling methods aggregate repeated samples. Self-consistency votes over independently sampled reasoning paths~\cite{wang2023selfconsistency}, while electoral aggregation seeks to preserve candidate diversity under alternative voting rules~\cite{zhao2024electoral}. These methods reduce single-sample variance, but normally treat every sample as an already-produced information object and do not distinguish the cost of a short answer, independent evidence production, or additional verification.

Multi-agent debate replaces independent sampling with interaction. Du et al. let model instances propose, exchange arguments, and revise answers over multiple rounds~\cite{du2024improving}; FORD studies inter-model consistency and domination by stronger agents~\cite{xiong2023interconsistency}; ChatEval, AgentVerse, and role-based systems organize debaters, critics, and evaluators into task workflows~\cite{chan2023chateval,chen2023agentverse}. Later work varies personas, reflection, debate modes, voting, and dynamic consensus~\cite{zhang2024collaboration,choi2025debate,li2025dynamic}. These studies primarily ask how produced outputs should communicate and aggregate. We move the decision boundary earlier and ask how an announced rule changes the action and resource path that produces a report.

\subsection{Conformity, Lost Dissent, and Communication Risk}
Interaction is not uniformly beneficial. Homogeneous model panels exhibit sycophancy, conformity, and premature consensus, allowing correct minority views to disappear~\cite{pitre2025consensagent,swamy2025peacemaker}; consensus and diversity show task-dependent trade-offs~\cite{wu2025disagreement}. Adversarial collaboration further demonstrates that a persuasive erroneous agent can contaminate peers through debate~\cite{amayuelas2024multiagent}. Communication rounds, topology, or majority agreement therefore do not by themselves establish informative deliberation. A system must also record who observed which reports, whether a stance change brought new evidence, and whether alternatives received comparable review.

Our confirmatory design holds the synchronous panel and information flow fixed while changing only the public pre-action rule. Controlled suites then expose new evidence, unsupported majority stances, copy pressure, or counterevidence. This complements failure-mode studies: prior work characterizes how errors propagate, whereas we test whether an observable process contract changes information production before propagation.

\subsection{Structured Reasoning, Verification, and Observable Reports}
Chain-of-Thought externalizes intermediate steps, Tree-of-Thoughts organizes search, and ReAct interleaves reasoning with tool actions~\cite{wei2022chain,yao2024tree,yao2023react}. They demonstrate the value of decomposed computation and tool grounding, but structured prose is not automatically factual and does not establish independent verification. Free-form reviews additionally suffer from missing fields, unnormalized conclusions, and untraceable evidence.

AEIC is an audit interface rather than a request for private chain-of-thought. It stores concise public assumptions, evidence, inference, and conclusion fields and converts completeness, legal stance, evidence increment, verified support, useful correction, and unsupported reversal into settlement signals. We separately compare free text, a generic analysis--answer structure, and AEIC under matched budgets so that benefits of structure in general are not attributed to the AEIC labels.

\subsection{Mechanism Design and Principal--Agent Views of LLMs}
Mechanism design studies rules committed before participants act. Existing work formulates auctions for LLM-mediated generated content~\cite{duetting2023mechanism}, uses statistical contracts to incentivize measurable text quality~\cite{saig2024incentivizing}, and frames multi-agent AI through principal--agent information asymmetry~\cite{rauba2025principal,kaltenpoth2025agency}. Stackelberg incentives are also used in federated learning, crowdsourcing, and resource allocation~\cite{fu2024stackelberg,guo2023incentive,wang2024incentive}.

Unlike retrospective scoring, our contract is visible to followers before solving, and the declared action selects different prompts, token caps, call counts, and verification paths. The empirical object is the induced action distribution of a black-box model under a fixed public contract, not an assumed human-like utility function. We use Stackelberg timing to organize a falsifiable experiment but do not claim a closed-form equilibrium, general incentive compatibility, or a globally optimal contract.

\subsection{Positioning}
Prior multi-agent work richly studies roles, topology, debate rounds, voting, and judges; structured reasoning provides intermediate representations; mechanism design provides commitment and response. The missing executable connection is that the rule must be visible before generation, the action must control a real resource path, the natural-language report must become a parseable settlement object, and retries and information exposure must remain traceable. We use AEIC as the reporting interface, a public contract plus action router as the treatment, and paired follower response as the primary evidence. The contribution therefore does not rely on assuming that debate always beats voting or that structured formatting is intrinsically accuracy improving.
