\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%======================================================================

\paragraph{What the experiments establish.} The primary result is behavioral and causal: revealing a Static-AEIC rule changes what followers choose to do, and the behavior change coincides with a large paired quality gain.  Outcome-only incentives are an essential control because they show that the effect cannot be reduced to offering a reward.

\paragraph{What they do not establish.} The experiments do not establish a dynamic Stackelberg optimum, universal necessity of the four AEIC fields, or cross-model superiority.  In particular, the held-out GLM result is not a nuisance to hide: it identifies a mechanism-design problem---how a contract's language and cost schedule map onto a particular backend's action policy.

\paragraph{Relation to topology.} Communication topology remains useful as an information and cost-control variable, but it is not the paper's causal centerpiece.  A topology can only route or filter the information agents produce.  AEIC-MD addresses the prior question of whether they produce a verifiable, costly report in the first place.  This ordering provides a principled way to retain prior topology assets without letting them displace the main contribution.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The primary formal comparison uses one backend and three QA domains.  The action menu and reward language are prompt-mediated implementations of costs and credits, rather than monetary transfers.  The boundary centre points are development data, and the held-out-backend result prevents a broad generalization claim.  Future work should pre-register backend-specific calibration, test mixed-model panels, and then study how an incentive-compatible reporting mechanism interacts with topology routing.
