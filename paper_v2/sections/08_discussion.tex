\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%======================================================================

\paragraph{Herding as a pathological attractor, not a bug of phrasing.}
Study~1 and the cross-model replication converge on a structural picture: multi-round LLM panels do not merely converge---they converge toward a model-specific attractor. For DeepSeek-V3 and R1 the attractor is rejection; for GLM-4-Flash it is acceptance with hedged language. The attractor's pull is strong enough that an explicit, direction-neutral moderation protocol (Study~2b) changes almost nothing: minorities instructed to persist unless specifically rebutted still capitulate at the same rate as unmoderated controls. This mirrors the QA-domain finding that static anti-sycophancy must act on mechanism \emph{parameters} (EV-weight suppression) rather than prompts, and extends it: even behavioral prompting fails when the underlying model prior is conformist. Effective regulation of LLM panels must therefore operate at the aggregation layer or the deliberation-structure layer, not the rhetoric layer.

\paragraph{A calibration-first doctrine.}
The clearest positive result is also the cheapest: the reject bias---a single-agent prior distortion---is repaired at aggregation time. The Stackelberg-optimized $\theta^*$ discovers this on its own, placing a $+1.04$ calibration term on the consensus score, and the simpler re-thresholded mean captures nearly the same agreement. The practical doctrine: before adding deliberation, calibrate the aggregation. Deliberation without calibration amplifies the prior's bias through the ratchet; calibrated independent aggregation already beats every discussion protocol we tested on decision agreement per call.

\paragraph{Discussion as an on-demand resource.}
Across two domains now, the same pattern appears: most instances need no discussion (70\% of panels are unanimous after round~1; on QA benchmarks, most questions are topology-insensitive), and unguided discussion can actively hurt. Routing converts this observation into cost savings: the review-domain router spends deliberation budget on only 17\% of papers and matches the best fixed protocol. The 10pp oracle gap marks the frontier---better prospective features (paper difficulty signals, reviewer disagreement semantics beyond entropy) could unlock it.

\paragraph{Behavioral health as the primary endpoint.}
A methodological contribution of this work is the endpoint doctrine. Decision agreement barely moved across any condition or intervention (all within 59--65\%), yet behavioral metrics moved by 50+ points with $p{<}10^{-28}$. If we had measured only decisions, Study~1 would have reported ``discussion is harmless''---exactly the wrong conclusion. Any evaluation of multi-agent judgment systems should report minority survival, variance preservation, and drift alongside accuracy, with accuracy demoted to a safety constraint. This is especially important when ground truth is noisy, as with official review decisions.

\paragraph{Transfer beyond peer review.}
The three layers map onto general multi-LLM judgment pipelines. Calibrated aggregation applies wherever multiple signals fuse into a decision---ranking ensembles in recommendation, multi-critic evaluation, red-team voting---wherever a shared prior bias needs a learned correction term. The behavioral health metrics (elimination rate, variance collapse, drift, flip asymmetry) constitute a \emph{deliberation health monitor} for multi-agent pipelines: they are computable from logged stances alone and could serve as production observability signals for agentic systems that deliberate before acting. Protocol routing generalizes to compute allocation: which queries merit multi-model deliberation and which should be answered by a single calibrated call. We have validated none of these transfers empirically; they are the natural next studies.

\paragraph{Relation to the QA-domain validation.}
Collective judgment poses two questions---\emph{is the decision right} and \emph{is the process healthy}---and the framework's two validations answer one each. On QA benchmarks, the same framework validated the routing layer (87.6\% accuracy, +4.5pp over the best fixed topology) and the structured-decomposition premise of AEIC (+28pp from explicit Conclusion fields), while its quality signals proved to measure coherence rather than correctness. The review domain changes the question: with noisy ground truth and behavioral stakes, the framework's value shifts from accuracy gains to diagnosis (making herding measurable), calibration (repairing prior bias), and cost-aware orchestration. The two validations are complementary: QA shows the framework optimizes what can be optimized; review shows it regulates what must be protected.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
(1)~Panels are homogeneous per model; heterogeneous panels (mixed models, mixed personas) may herd differently---our three-model study samples backends but does not mix them within a panel.
(2)~The official decision is noisy ground truth; we mitigate via the safety-endpoint doctrine and per-stratum reporting, but absolute agreement values should be read as ``agreement with human panels,'' not correctness.
(3)~GLM stances are 94\% rating-derived (hedged conclusions), so its behavioral metrics partly reflect rating dynamics.
(4)~Study~2b tests one specific prompt-level protocol; stronger mechanism-level regulations (e.g., aggregation-time minority protection, structured rebuttal rounds) remain untested.
(5)~The router's features are round-1 statistics; prospective difficulty estimation is shallow, and the oracle gap suggests substantial headroom.
(6)~All datasets derive from one venue and year (ICLR~2024); cross-venue generality is open.
