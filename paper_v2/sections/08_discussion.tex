\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%======================================================================

\paragraph{Why Stackelberg over Nash?}
Yi et al.~\cite{yi2025debate} model multi-agent debate as a simultaneous-move Bayesian game. Our Stackelberg formulation is more natural for mechanism design: the aggregation rule is \emph{committed to} before agents act, enabling the leader to shape follower behavior through credible commitment---a property absent in Nash equilibrium where all players move simultaneously.

\paragraph{Quality Signals vs.\ Correctness.}
Our most significant finding is that AEIC quality signals measure reasoning process quality, not answer correctness. McNemar tests confirm that AEIC+SG weighted voting is significantly \emph{worse} than majority voting on three topologies (parallel $p{=}0.010$, sequential $p{=}0.004$, debate $p{=}0.003$) and only significantly better on hierarchical ($p{=}0.017$). A coherent, well-evidenced wrong answer scores as highly as a correct one. This implies that quality-based weighting cannot substitute for diversity-based aggregation when agents share the same LLM backend and similar knowledge biases.

\paragraph{AEIC as Feature Extraction.}
Despite limited value as direct voting weights, AEIC signals are informative features for topology routing. Feature ablation reveals that voting features (majority ratio, yes/no distribution) contribute the most to routing accuracy ($-2.1$pp), while EQ-related features are harmful (removing them \emph{improves} accuracy by up to 1.6pp). This parallels the AEIC+SG ablation findings: evidence quality signals introduce noise at both the weighting and routing levels. The router achieves 87.6\% using these features with top-1 selection, suggesting that the appropriate level for quality assessment in multi-agent systems is \emph{meta-level topology selection} rather than agent-level vote weighting.

\paragraph{Anti-Sycophancy from First Principles.}
Optimal mechanisms learn near-zero EV weights on star and debate topologies, providing a principled anti-sycophancy mechanism. On information-sharing topologies, high EV reflects social influence rather than independent verification. This emerges from accuracy optimization without explicit constraints, complementing heuristic approaches~\cite{pitre2025consensagent} with a game-theoretic foundation.

\paragraph{Oracle Gap Analysis.}
The 5.3pp gap between routing (87.6\%) and oracle (92.9\%) arises from questions where no topology produces correct consensus (7.0\%) and routing errors where the selected topology fails despite a correct alternative existing. Analyzing topology agreement: on 62.1\% of questions, all five topologies agree (55.1\% all correct, 7.0\% all wrong); routing adds no value here. The router's impact comes from the 9.0\% of ``hard'' questions where only 1--2 topologies succeed. Our router closes 46.5\% of the gap between the best fixed topology (83.1\%) and the oracle. The remaining gap could be reduced by richer topology spaces, heterogeneous agent pools, or improved routing features that better distinguish question difficulty patterns.

\paragraph{Comparison with RL-Based Adaptive Topology.}
RUMAD~\cite{abdelnabi2026rumad} uses reinforcement learning to adaptively control debate rounds and agent participation within a single topology type. CARD~\cite{liu2026card} routes across debate configurations. Our approach differs in three ways: (1)~we select among five fundamentally different communication structures (parallel, sequential, star, debate, hierarchical), not just debate variants; (2)~our routing is based on interpretable AEIC quality signals with game-theoretic grounding, while RUMAD uses opaque RL policies; (3)~our Stackelberg mechanism provides formal incentive compatibility guarantees (Theorem~\ref{thm:ic}). The approaches are complementary---within-topology adaptation (RUMAD) and cross-topology selection (ours) could be combined.

\paragraph{Online Stackelberg Game.}
Our online extension injects quality feedback between rounds, transforming Stackelberg from post-hoc reweighting to real-time incentive shaping. The experimental results (Table~\ref{tab:online}) reveal that effectiveness depends critically on topology--task interaction: debate benefits strongly on knowledge-intensive tasks (+6.7pp on MMLU-Hard, confirmed at +5.4pp with $n{=}74$) and shows moderate gains on commonsense reasoning (+2.4pp on StrategyQA, $n{=}41$); hierarchical shows no improvement ($-5.4$pp average with expanded validation). This suggests that online feedback is most valuable when (1)~the topology supports genuine iterative refinement and (2)~the task rewards deeper reasoning rather than resistance to misleading cues. The mixed results motivate adaptive feedback policies that modulate signal injection based on task characteristics.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
(1)~The router requires running all five topologies for feature extraction (5$\times$ cost); online deployment needs topology prediction from question features alone.
(2)~All agents use the same LLM (DeepSeek-V3); heterogeneous pools may behave differently.
(3)~Online Stackelberg feedback shows mixed results: beneficial on MMLU-Hard debate but degrading on TruthfulQA and hierarchical, suggesting the need for task-adaptive feedback policies.
(4)~The online evaluation uses 30--74 questions per condition (API availability limited some conditions), with the largest effects ($\pm$5--7pp) reaching marginal significance.
(5)~Question sets are not fully shared across topologies (only 633 of 900 questions overlap across all five), limiting the router evaluation set.
