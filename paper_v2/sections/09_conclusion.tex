\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%======================================================================

We presented an adaptive game-theoretic framework for multi-agent LLM consensus that combines AEIC reasoning decomposition, Stackelberg incentive mechanisms with online feedback, and neural topology routing. On 633 questions evaluated across all five communication topologies on three reasoning benchmarks with 5 agents, our per-dataset router achieves 87.6\% accuracy (+4.5pp over the best fixed topology on aligned questions), demonstrating that AEIC-structured outputs enable effective adaptive topology selection through reliable cross-topology voting-pattern comparison.

Our key findings include: (1)~AEIC quality signals measure reasoning coherence rather than answer correctness---AEIC+SG is significantly worse than majority voting on 3 of 5 topologies (McNemar $p{<}0.05$); structured output ablation confirms AEIC's primary value: stance extraction from the explicit Conclusion~field achieves +28.0pp higher majority voting accuracy than from unstructured Inference text (76.9\% vs.\ 48.9\%), and the structured format enables reliable cross-topology consensus; (2)~per-topology Stackelberg optimization discovers interpretable parameter profiles, including anti-sycophancy effects where cross-agent consensus signals are suppressed on information-sharing topologies; (3)~the mechanism naturally achieves high incentive compatibility (94--100\% on 4 of 5 topologies) without explicit IC constraints; and (4)~the Stackelberg mechanism evolves through three layers: static optimization discovers anti-sycophancy principles, online feedback improves debate accuracy on knowledge-intensive tasks (+5.4pp on MMLU-Hard with $n{=}74$), and dynamic sycophancy regulation---monitoring collective stance dynamics (256 transitions analyzed) and applying adaptive interventions---achieves the best accuracy on knowledge-intensive reasoning (94.0\% on MMLU-Hard, +8pp over both baseline and static at $n{=}50$), while maintaining baseline accuracy on other tasks where uniform static feedback degrades it, demonstrating the value of selective behavioral regulation over uniform quality-based weighting.

Future work includes replacing embedding similarity with NLI-based entailment scoring---our preliminary experiments suggest that cross-agent NLI calibration combined with heterogeneous agent pools may overcome the self-consistency ceiling identified in this work---developing online routing that selects topologies from question features without running all candidates, exploring heterogeneous agent pools with diverse LLM backends where quality signals may become more discriminative, refining dynamic regulation thresholds through larger-scale validation, and combining cross-topology selection with within-topology adaptation~\cite{wang2026rumad}.
