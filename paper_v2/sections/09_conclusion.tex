\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%======================================================================

We presented an adaptive game-theoretic framework for multi-agent LLM consensus that combines AEIC reasoning decomposition, Stackelberg incentive mechanisms with online feedback, and neural topology routing. On 633 questions evaluated across all five communication topologies on three reasoning benchmarks with 5 agents, our per-dataset router achieves 87.6\% accuracy (+4.5pp over the best fixed topology on aligned questions), demonstrating that AEIC-derived features enable effective adaptive topology selection.

Our key findings include: (1)~AEIC quality signals measure reasoning process quality rather than answer correctness---AEIC+SG is significantly worse than majority voting on 3 of 5 topologies (McNemar $p{<}0.05$)---making them more valuable as topology routing features than as direct voting weights; (2)~per-topology Stackelberg optimization discovers interpretable parameter profiles, including anti-sycophancy effects where cross-agent consensus signals are suppressed on information-sharing topologies; (3)~the mechanism naturally achieves high incentive compatibility (94--100\% on 4 of 5 topologies) without explicit IC constraints; and (4)~online Stackelberg feedback between rounds can substantially improve debate accuracy on knowledge-intensive tasks (+5.4pp on MMLU-Hard with $n{=}74$), though the effect is topology- and task-dependent.

Future work includes developing online routing that selects topologies from question features without running all candidates, evaluating the online Stackelberg game with live LLM agents, exploring heterogeneous agent pools with diverse LLM backends, and combining cross-topology selection with within-topology adaptation~\cite{abdelnabi2026rumad}.
